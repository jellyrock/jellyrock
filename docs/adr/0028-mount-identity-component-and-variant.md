# ADR 0028: A mount's identity is component AND variant, and one selection rule serves every reader

**Status:** Accepted
**Date:** 2026-08-14

**Partially supersedes:** [ADR 0027](0027-screen-readiness-ledger.md) (mount-selection clause only — the ledger, the fill model and the derived milestones are unchanged)

**related-files**: `scripts/measure-selection.js`, `scripts/measure.js`, `scripts/measure-loop.js`, `scripts/measure-args.js`, `scripts/measure-compare.js`, `tests/scripts/unit/measure-selection.test.js`, `tests/scripts/unit/measure-compare.test.js`

[ADR 0027](0027-screen-readiness-ledger.md) established that a launch mounting several screens publishes no median "until `--variant` names one". That clause is too narrow, and the narrowness is invisible until measurement reaches a screen behind playback. There are two ways a navigation walks through a screen on the way to the one asked for, and each is blind to the other's discriminator. A **chained** nav mounts ONE component several times — reaching a Season loads its Series first — and `variant` separates them. A **playback** nav mounts DIFFERENT components — reaching the player means walking through `ItemDetails` — and for a movie both stamp variant `Movie`, so `variant` sees one value and concludes the launch was single-mount. Measured on `.177`: `--nav osd` skipped the refusal entirely, took the first mount by position, and published the details screen's **300 ms** paint under `screen: osd` while the player's own samples in the same record read 2811 and 3471 ms. `--variant Movie` could not have rescued it — it matches the details screen first. So a mount's identity is the PAIR, `--component` joins `--variant` as a checkable selector (refused when no sample carried it, unlike the asserted `--screen`), and ambiguity is counted in distinct identities rather than in samples: one identity mounted twice in a launch is a later run, not a rival, and refusing it would demand a flag that cannot exist.

The second half is where the real defect lived. The rule "which sample is THE sample" was implemented three times — the per-launch progress line (since moved to `scripts/measure-loop.js` as `formatLaunchLine`, still calling the one rule), the series summary, and `measure-compare.js`'s `coldSamples()` — and fixing the publisher alone left the reader selecting by position, so `measure` published 2135 ms and `measure:compare` read 254 ms back off the same record. A tool whose entire purpose is deciding whether two numbers differ cannot have two answers to which number it is looking at, so the rule now lives once, in `scripts/measure-selection.js`, and every entry point calls it. That module is deliberately pure — no device, no ledger — because `measure.js` claims the Roku on import and therefore cannot be unit-tested, which is the same reason `measure-args.js` was carved out and a stronger one here: argument parsing never had a defect, and this layer has now had four, every one found by hand on hardware. Two consequences are recorded rather than left to be rediscovered. A selector only applies when the samples can answer it — `home-latest-rows` (what bare `npm run measure` samples) and `item-grid` emit purely numeric lines, so `splitWorkload` gives them no dimensions and they must keep selecting by position or a `--variant` would silently empty the series. And `component` joins `measure:compare`'s mixing gate: until measurement reached playback, `screen` implied the component, and without the gate an `itemDetails` arm and a `videoPlayer` arm both carrying `screen: osd` were compared with the workload line actively reassuring the reader that they were doing identical work.

**Later:** `variant` above is described as what KIND of load a mount was — an item type, a library type — because every mount this ADR had seen was one of a screen's several *kinds*. It also separates a screen's several *loads*: `search` mounts once and loads twice (a keyboard, then a query), and the two are separate ledger runs stamped `open` and `query`. That is the same job under this ADR's own rule — separating one component's samples so a reader can name the one they meant — and it needed no new field and no tooling change. Recorded here because a narrow reading of the paragraph above would call it a misuse of `variant` and "correct" it out of the next multi-load screen. See decision [`screen-with-two-loads-is-two-runs`](../decisions.md).
